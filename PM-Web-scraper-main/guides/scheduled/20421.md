# Scheduled Maintenance Procedure: Advance lift (EQ ID 5370)

## Overview
The Advance lift P‑2536 relies on a clean hydraulic system and properly seated mechanical components to raise and lower loads safely. Contaminated fluid, debris in the pit, or worn snap‑rings/rollers can cause jamming, loss of lift capacity, or sudden failures that jeopardize operator safety and result in costly downtime. This preventive maintenance routine verifies fluid condition, removes foreign material, and confirms that all moving parts and safety devices are in good working order, thereby preserving lift performance and protecting personnel.

## Tools & Parts Needed
- PPE: safety glasses, cut‑ resistant gloves, steel‑toe boots, hearing protection, high‑visibility vest  
- Standard hand tools: socket set, open/torque wrenches, screwdrivers, pliers, hex key set  
- Flashlight or inspection lamp  
- Clean lint‑free rags and shop towels  
- Automotive‑grade hydraulic fluid (type specified in the OEM manual)  
- Brake‑clean or equivalent solvent for cleaning fittings and contacts  
- Lockout/Tagout lock(s) and padlock(s)  
- Portable vacuum or shop‑floor broom  

## Safety
- **Lockout/Tagout**: De‑energize the lift by disconnecting the main power, engage the service disconnect switch, and apply lockout/tagout devices to the power breaker.  
- **Release Stored Energy**: Bleed hydraulic pressure from the system using the service valve before starting any work inside the pit or near cylinders.  
- **Pit Access**: Use a certified fall‑protection harness and a secure ladder or platform when working inside the pit.  
- **Fluid Handling**: Wear gloves and goggles when checking or topping up hydraulic fluid to avoid skin contact.  
- **Electrical Caution**: Verify that all electrical lines are de‑energized before inspecting or touching wiring or connectors.

## Step‑by‑Step Procedure
1. Verify that the lift is fully lowered, the main power is disconnected, and lockout/tagout devices are in place.  
2. Open the hydraulic reservoir cap and clean the surrounding area with a rag to prevent contamination.  
3. Check the fluid level using the dip‑stick; the fluid should be within the “MAX” and “MIN” marks. Add the correct OEM‑specified hydraulic fluid if the level is low.  
4. Inspect the fluid’s appearance: it should be clear to amber, free of metal particles, water, or foam. If cloudy, milky, or contains debris, schedule a fluid change.  
5. Sweep and vacuum the floor and pit around the lift’s underside, removing any debris, metal shavings, oil, or foreign objects that could interfere with rollers or the cylinder rod.  
6. Locate all snap rings and retaining clips on the axle housings, cylinder mounts, and roller assemblies; ensure each ring is fully seated in its groove and not deformed.  
7. Visually examine each roller, pin, and bushing: rotate the roller by hand, looking for flat spots, scoring, excessive play, or missing fasteners. Good condition = smooth, quiet rotation with no visible wear.  
8. Inspect each hydraulic fitting (male/female connections, nipple, and cylinder ports) for cracks, corrosion, or oil seepage. Wipe any weepage with a solvent‑dampened rag; tighten any loose fittings per OEM torque recommendations.  
9. Examine all hydraulic hoses and electrical wiring for abrasions, cuts, kinks, or chafed insulation. Confirm that hose clamps are tight and that electrical connectors are snug and free of corrosion.  
10. Re‑connect power (remove lockout devices only after the above inspections are complete) and operate the lift through a full travel cycle at no load. Listen for abnormal noises (whining, grinding) and feel for excessive vibration or uneven motion.  
11. Test each safety device:  
    - Pull the emergency stop to verify the lift stops instantly.  
    - Inspect the pleated bellows for cracks or tears; they must be intact and fully expanded.  
    - Operate the electric toe guard; it should retract and extend smoothly without binding.  
    - Verify limit switches engage at the proper top and bottom positions (observe indicator lights or audible cues).  
12. After the test run, lower the lift, bleed any residual pressure, and re‑inspect the fluid level once more; top up if necessary.  
13. Clean the work area, dispose of used rags and solvent containers according to site environmental procedures, and remove lockout/tagout devices to return the lift to service.

## Completion Checklist
- [ ] Hydraulic fluid level is correct and fluid appears clean.  
- [ ] All debris cleared from floor and pit area.  
- [ ] Snap rings and clips are present, undamaged, and fully seated.  
- [ ] Rollers, pins, and bushings show no wear, flat spots, or missing fasteners.  
- [ ] Hydraulic fittings are crack‑free, leak‑free, and properly tightened.  
- [ ] Hoses and electrical lines are free of abrasion, cuts, and have secure connections.  
- [ ] Lift operates through full travel without abnormal noise or vibration.  
- [ ] Safety devices (emergency stop, pleated bellows, electric toe guard, limit switches) function correctly.  
- [ ] All lockout/tagout devices removed and lift returned to normal operation.
