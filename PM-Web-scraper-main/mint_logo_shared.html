MINT — partially layered (shared strokes)

The letters are written out left-to-right, but neighbours share a vertical stroke: the right leg of the M is also the I and the left leg of the N. Shown large so you can see the joins; each also renders on dark.

Option A — Monoline connected

One shared vertical does triple duty (M-right / I / N-left). Thin, even strokes. The single-color version is what most people would use as the wordmark.

monoline · one shared vertical

Option B — Two-tone (reveals the trick)

Same geometry, but each letter is tinted differently so the shared vertical (drawn in the darkest shade) visibly belongs to M, I and N at once. Nice for a splash/marketing moment; can collapse to one color for everyday use.

two-tone · highlights shared stroke

Option C — Bold solid

Heavier weight for presence in the header / favicon. Same shared-vertical construction, thicker strokes and slightly tighter spacing.

bold · header ready

Option D — Maximum overlap

Goes further: N's right leg also becomes the stem of the T (crossbar sits on top). Just two "gaps" total — the most compact, most layered read of MINT.
